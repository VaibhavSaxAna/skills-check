P1

Order verified, hands clean, supplies out

At the cart · steps 1–2

Checklist says1 Verified the health care provider's orders. 2 Performed hand hygiene and prepared supplies.
  1. 1.1

    Order against the MAR

    #1
    DO
    Order beside the MAR. Finger on each line of the order, then the matching line on the MAR.
    SAY
    "Verifying the provider's order against the MAR. Patient — [name], date of birth matches. Medication — [drug], [drug]. Dose — [dose], [dose]. Route — IM, ventrogluteal. Time — [time], [frequency]. The order is complete and matches."
  2. ✋

    Hand hygiene, then supplies on the cart top

    #2
    DO
    Hand hygiene first. Then lay out: MAR · vial of medication · vial of diluent · alcohol swabs · syringe for diluent · syringe and needle for the dose · filter needle if the manufacturer calls for one · syringe label · gloves · gauze · bandage · pen and paper for the math.
    SAY
    "Performing hand hygiene. Preparing my supplies: MAR, the medication vial, the diluent, alcohol swabs, syringes and needles, a label, gloves, gauze and a bandage."
P2

Select the vial, read it, do the math

At the cart · steps 3–5

Checklist says3 Selected vial of the prescribed drug from the medication cart or automated dispensing system. Compared the name of the drug on the label of the vial with the name in the MAR. 4 Checked the expiration date and drug concentration or dose on the vial. Calculated the correct volume to withdraw. 5 Articulates that the RN needs to check agency policy to note which high-alert medications require validation by a second RN and an independent double-checked calculation.
  1. 2.1

    Vial out, name to MAR

    #3
    DO
    Take the client's vial from the cart or dispensing system. Hold the label beside the MAR entry.
    SAY
    "Selecting [drug] from the cart. Comparing the drug name on the vial to the MAR — [drug], [drug]. They match."
  2. 2.2

    Expiry, concentration, and the calculation on paper

    #4
    DO
    Turn the vial, find the expiry. Read the strength on the label. Write dose ordered ÷ concentration = volume, and say it as you write it.
    SAY
    "Expiry date [date] — not expired. The vial contains [amount] per vial. Ordered dose is [dose]. [dose] over [concentration], times 1 mL, equals [volume] mL to withdraw."
  3. 2.3

    High-alert policy, said out loud

    #5
    DO
    This step is graded only on what the evaluator hears. Say it before you touch the diluent.
    SAY
    "I would check agency policy to see whether this is a high-alert medication. If it is, it requires validation by a second RN and an independent double-check of my calculation before I draw it up."
P3

Reconstitute the powder

At the cart · steps 6–10

Checklist says6 Removed the cap on the vial of powdered medication and the cap on a vial of the proper diluent. Made sure to check the manufacturer's guideline for the correct diluent and volume of diluent to use, and if use of a filter needle was recommended. 7 Wiped both rubber seals with an alcohol swab, and allowed the alcohol to dry. 8 Used a needle or needleless device to draw the appropriate type and volume of diluent suggested by the manufacturer into the syringe. 9 Inserted the tip of the needle or the needleless device through the center of the rubber seal of the vial of powdered medication. Injected the measured diluent into the vial containing the powder. 10 Rolled the vial between the palms of hands to mix the powder and diluents thoroughly. Did not shake the vial.
  1. 3.1

    Caps off both vials; manufacturer's guideline read

    #6
    DO
    Flip the plastic cap off the powder vial and off the diluent vial. Pick up the package insert or read the label and name the three things the guideline tells you.
    SAY
    "Removing the caps from the medication vial and the diluent vial. Per the manufacturer's guideline: the diluent is [sterile water / 0.9% NaCl], the volume is [x] mL, and a filter needle [is / is not] recommended."
  2. 3.2

    Both seals swabbed, air dried

    #7
    DO
    One swab per seal. Firm circles. Set the vials down and keep your hands off the stoppers while they dry.
    SAY
    "Wiping both rubber seals with alcohol and allowing them to dry."
  3. 3.3

    Draw the diluent: the right type, the right volume

    #8
    DO
    Syringe with needle or needleless device. Draw air equal to the diluent volume, inject it into the diluent vial, invert, and draw back exactly the volume from 3.1. Clear bubbles inside the vial. Needle out.
    SAY
    "Drawing up [x] mL of [diluent], the type and volume the manufacturer suggests."
  4. 3.4

    Through the centre of the seal; diluent in

    #9
    DO
    Powder vial upright on the cart. Needle tip through the centre of the stopper at 90°. Push the plunger slowly so the diluent runs down the inside wall.
    SAY
    "Inserting through the centre of the rubber seal and injecting [x] mL of diluent into the powder."
  5. 3.5

    Roll between the palms. Never shake.

    #10
    DO
    Withdraw the needle first. Vial between flat palms, roll back and forth until the solution is clear with no particles. Hold it to the light.
    SAY
    "Rolling the vial between my palms to mix — not shaking, so it doesn't foam. The solution is clear."
P4

Read the new concentration, draw the dose

At the cart · steps 11–12

Checklist says11 Read the label carefully to determine the concentration of the drug. 12 Wiped off the top with an alcohol swab and allowed it to dry. Drew up the appropriate volume of medication based on its concentration in the reconstituted formulation into a syringe.
  1. 4.1

    Concentration after reconstitution, math written again

    #11
    DO
    Read the label's "after reconstitution" line. Write the calculation a second time with that concentration.
    SAY
    "Reading the label: after reconstitution with [x] mL, the concentration is [mg] per mL. [dose] over [mg/mL], times 1 mL, equals [volume] mL."
  2. 4.2

    Swab the top again, dry, draw the volume

    #12
    DO
    New alcohol swab on the stopper. Let it dry. New syringe (and filter needle if 3.1 said so). Air equal to the dose in, invert, draw to the volume from 4.1, clear bubbles in the closed system, needle out. If you used a filter needle, change to the injection needle now. Scoop-recap on a flat surface.
    SAY
    "Wiping the top with alcohol and letting it dry. Drawing up [volume] mL based on the reconstituted concentration. Bubbles cleared — [volume] mL in the syringe."
P5

Label checks, equipment, CNO standard, privacy

At the cart, then walking in · steps 13–15

Checklist says13 Checked the medication label twice against the order in the medication administration record. Made sure to check the medication expiration date when retrieving the medication. 14 Gathered the necessary equipment and supplies. Followed CNO medication standard. 15 Provided for the patient's privacy.
  1. 5.1

    Label to MAR, twice, with expiry

    #13
    DO
    Vial and filled syringe beside the MAR. Point through name, drug, dose, route, time once. Then say "second check" and do it again. Name the expiry in the first pass.
    SAY
    "First label check against the MAR: [name], [drug], [dose] which is [volume] mL, IM, [time]. Expiry [date], not expired. Second label check: [name], [drug], [dose], [volume] mL, IM, [time]. Matches."
  2. 5.2

    Label the syringe, name the equipment, name the standard

    #14
    DO
    Write the label (client, drug, dose, your initials and designation, date, time) and wrap it so the volume stays readable. Vial back, cart locked. Tray: MAR · labelled syringe · two alcohol swabs · gauze · gloves · bandage · sharps access.
    SAY
    "Label applied. I have my MAR, the labelled syringe, alcohol swabs, gauze, gloves and a bandage. I'm following the CNO Medication practice standard: the rights of medication administration, three checks, and documentation immediately after."
    Say "CNO medication standard." The step is graded on those words being heard, not on you listing every right.
  3. 5.3

    Knock, curtain, door

    #15
    DO

    SAY
    "Providing privacy — closing the curtain."

P7

Choose the site, inspect it, position the patient

Bedside · steps 21–23

Checklist says21 Selected an appropriate injection site. Noted the integrity and size of the patient's muscle. Palpated for tenderness or hardness, and avoided such areas. If the patient received frequent injections, rotated the site selection. 22 Inspected the area for bruising, inflammation, or edema. If the skin was bruised or showed signs of infection, used a different site. 23 Helped the patient into a comfortable position, according to the site being used. Exposed only that portion of the body.
  1. 7.1

    Site named, muscle noted, palpated, rotation asked

    #21
    DO
    Say the site and why. Look at the muscle mass. Press over the area with your fingertips. Ask about recent injections and which side.
    SAY
    "The site is the ventrogluteal — the gluteus medius. It's the safest IM site: thick muscle, away from major nerves and vessels, and it takes up to 3 mL. The muscle is adequate in size and integrity. Palpating — no tenderness or hardness. Have you had any injections recently? Which side? … I'll rotate to the [other] side."
  2. 7.2

    Skin inspected: bruising, inflammation, edema, infection

    #22
    DO
    Look at the skin before gloves go on. Name each thing you're ruling out.
    SAY
    "Inspecting the site — no bruising, no inflammation, no edema, no signs of infection. If there were, I'd choose a different site."
  3. 7.3

    Side-lying, knee flexed, hip exposed only

    #23
    DO
    Help the client onto their side with the upper knee bent and brought forward over the lower leg, which relaxes the gluteus medius. Fold the gown or drape back to show only the hip.
    SAY
    "I'll help you onto your side, top knee bent forward — that relaxes the muscle. I'm only uncovering your hip."

P10

Z-track injection

Bedside · eight elements in this order · steps 36–43

Checklist says36 Positioned the ulnar side of nondominant hand just below the injection site, and pulled the patient's skin laterally 2.5 to 3.5 cm. Held this position until needle was inserted. 37 Grasped the body of the muscle between thumb and forefinger. 38 With dominant hand, quickly pierced the muscle at a 90-degree angle. 39 After the needle had pierced the muscle, continued to pull the skin taut with nondominant hand. Stabilized the syringe by grasping the lower end of the barrel with the fingers of nondominant hand as they were resting on the patient. 40 Articulates that aspiration is required for dorsogluteal site only and if blood appeared in the syringe repeat procedure with a new syringe and needle. 41 Injected the medication. 42 Waited 10 seconds, and then smoothly and steadily withdrew the needle. Engaged the needle safety sheath. Released the skin and applied a gauze pad over the site. 43 Applied gentle pressure. Did not massage the site. Applied a bandage to the injection site if needed.
  1. 10.1

    Ulnar side of the hand below the site, pull skin 2.5–3.5 cm, hold

    #36
    DO
    Pinky edge of your non-dominant hand sits just below the alcohol-wipe mark. Drag the skin sideways about an inch and keep it there. Do not let go.
    SAY
    "Z-track: ulnar side of my non-dominant hand below the site, pulling the skin laterally 2.5 to 3.5 centimetres and holding it."
  2. 10.2

    Grasp the body of the muscle

    #37
    DO
    With the skin still displaced, thumb and forefinger of the same hand grasp the muscle belly to firm it up.
    SAY
    "Grasping the body of the muscle between thumb and forefinger."
  3. 10.3

    Dart in at 90°

    #38
    DO
    One quick, smooth motion, perpendicular to the skin, to the hub.
    SAY
    "Piercing the muscle quickly at a 90-degree angle."
  4. 10.4

    Keep the skin taut; stabilize the barrel with the hand resting on the patient

    #39
    DO
    The displaced skin stays displaced. The fingers of that same non-dominant hand reach up and grip the lower end of the barrel while the heel of the hand rests on the hip. Nothing wobbles.
    SAY
    "Skin still pulled taut. Stabilizing the syringe at the lower end of the barrel with my non-dominant hand resting on the patient."
  5. 10.5

    Aspiration, said not done

    #40
    DO
    Do not pull back on the plunger at the ventrogluteal. Say the rule instead.
    SAY
    "Aspiration is only required at the dorsogluteal site, so I'm not aspirating here. If I were and blood appeared in the syringe, I would withdraw, discard, and repeat the procedure with a new syringe and needle."
  6. 10.6

    Inject slowly

    #41
    DO
    Dominant thumb on the plunger, about 1 mL per 10 seconds, even pressure.
    SAY
    "Injecting the medication slowly."
  7. 10.7

    Count 10, withdraw smoothly, safety on, release skin, gauze on

    #42
    DO
    Count out loud to ten with the needle still in. Pull straight out at 90°, no wiggle. Flip the safety sheath down one-handed against a firm surface. Only now release the skin, so the track seals. Gauze from between your fingers goes onto the site.
    SAY
    "Waiting 10 seconds — one, two … ten. Withdrawing smoothly and steadily. Engaging the safety sheath. Releasing the skin — the Z-track seals the medication in. Gauze over the site."
  8. 10.8

    Gentle pressure, no massage, bandage if needed

    #43
    DO
    Hold the gauze with light pressure. Do not rub. Look for bleeding; a small bandage if there is any.
    SAY
    "Gentle pressure with gauze — not massaging, which would force the medication into the tissue. Applying a bandage since there's a spot of blood."
P11

Sharps, gloves off, document, watch for reaction

Bedside · steps 44–47

Checklist says44 Discarded the needle and its attached syringe in a puncture-proof, leak-proof container. 45 Helped the patient into a comfortable position. Disposed of used gauze. Removed gloves and performed hand hygiene. 46 Documented the injection immediately in the MAR. Included the medication name, the dosage given, and the site in which it was injected. 47 Stayed with the patient for several minutes, observing for any allergic reaction.
  1. 11.1

    Whole syringe into the sharps

    #44
    DO
    Needle and syringe together, straight into the sharps container. Never set it down on the bed first.
    SAY
    "Needle and syringe directly into the puncture-proof, leak-proof sharps container."
  2. 11.2

    Reposition, gauze to garbage, gloves off, hand hygiene

    #45
    DO
    Help the client back onto their back, gown down. Gauze in the garbage. Gloves off glove-to-glove then skin-to-skin, into the garbage. Hand hygiene.
    SAY
    "Let's get you comfortable again. Disposing of the gauze. Removing gloves. Performing hand hygiene."
  3. 11.3

    Sign the MAR now: name, dose, site

    #46
    DO
    Pen on the MAR in the room, before anything else. Date, time, initials in the administration box; site in the site box; initials, full signature and designation in the legend.
    SAY
    "Documenting immediately in the MAR: [drug], [dose] given, [left] ventrogluteal, [time], my initials — and my full signature and designation in the legend."
  4. 11.4

    Stay several minutes, watching for a reaction

    #47
    DO
    Stay at the bedside. Look at the face and the site. Ask how they feel.
    SAY
    "I'll stay with you for a few minutes to watch for any allergic reaction — any itching, rash, swelling, trouble breathing, or feeling faint? … Good."

Study guide
one goal per phase

Each skill is a chain of phases. A phase has one goal, a clear starting point, and a clear finish line. The finish line of one phase is the starting point of the next, so if you know what the end looks like, you always know where to pick up.

How to use this tonight
  1. Read the chain at the top of the skill once. That's the whole skill in nine lines.
  2. For each phase, read Start and End first, then the steps between. Ask: what has to be true before I can move on?
  3. Tick Hide the steps. Look only at Start → End and say what happens in between, out loud. Tap a phase to check.
  4. Then run Test your understanding on the full checklist page.
The chain
  1. Check the order
  2. Pick the vial, do the math
  3. Mix the powder
  4. Draw up and label
  5. Walk in and connect
  6. Choose the site and position
  7. Landmark and prep the skin
  8. Z-track injection
  9. Make them safe and sign

The thread: everything before the door is about making one labelled syringe you trust. Everything after the door is about one safe injection into a site you found with your hand.

1

Check the order

Goal: know exactly what you're giving, to whom, and that your hands are clean.
Start Order and MAR in front of you at the cart. Nothing touched yet.
Steps
  • Order to MAR, line by line: patient, drug, dose, route, time. "They match."
  • Hand hygiene.
  • Lay out supplies: MAR, med vial, diluent, swabs, syringes and needles, filter needle if the insert says so, label, gloves, gauze, bandage, pen.
End Order verified, hands clean, everything on the cart top.
Why: every later check compares back to the MAR. If this is wrong, every check after it is wrong.
2

Pick the vial, do the math

Goal: the right vial, in date, and a written volume to draw.
Start Supplies out. Vial still in the cart.
Steps
  • Take the vial. Name on the vial to name on the MAR.
  • Read the expiry out loud. Read the strength on the label.
  • Write it: dose ordered ÷ concentration = mL. Say it as you write it.
  • Say the rule: "If this were high-alert, agency policy needs a second RN and an independent double-check."
End Right vial in hand, expiry read, math on paper, high-alert rule said.
Why: the math is graded on paper. Say it, write it, keep the paper.
3

Mix the powder

Goal: a clear solution whose concentration you've read off the label.
Start Powder vial and diluent vial, both capped.
Steps
  • Caps off both. Read the insert: which diluent, how much, filter needle or not.
  • Swab both stoppers. Let them dry.
  • Draw the diluent (air in, invert, draw, bubbles out).
  • Needle through the centre of the powder vial's stopper. Push the diluent in slowly.
  • Needle out. Roll between palms until clear. Never shake.
  • Read the label again: "after reconstitution, X mg per mL". Redo the math with that number.
End One clear vial of mixed drug; the new concentration read; the math written a second time.
Why: the diluent volume sets the concentration. The number you draw comes from the label's "after reconstitution" line, not the powder strength.
4

Draw up and label

Goal: one labelled syringe holding exactly the dose, and a locked cart.
Start Mixed vial, new syringe.
Steps
  • Swab the stopper again, let it dry. Air in, invert, draw to the volume, bubbles out in the vial, needle out. Scoop-recap on a flat surface.
  • Label to MAR: name, drug, dose, route, time, expiry. Then say "second check" and do it again.
  • Label the syringe: patient, drug, dose, your initials and designation, date, time. Wrap so the volume still shows.
  • Name your tray: MAR, labelled syringe, two swabs, gauze, gloves, bandage.
  • Say: "I'm following the CNO Medication practice standard."
  • Vial back. Cart locked.
End Labelled syringe on a tray with the MAR; two label checks done; cart locked; standard named.
Why: the words "CNO medication standard" are a tick on their own. Say them here, once.
5

Walk in and connect

Goal: the right patient, who knows what's coming and has agreed.
Start At the door with the tray.
Steps
  • Knock. Curtain closed. Hand hygiene. "Hi, I'm ___, your student nurse." Greet family.
  • Two identifiers: name and date of birth, from them, from the band, against the MAR. Allergies the same three ways.
  • Third check at the bedside: syringe label to MAR to the patient's name.

End Patient identified three ways, allergies confirmed, third check done, consent given, sting warned.
Why: step 20 has seven graded pieces in one sentence. Practise that sentence until it comes out whole.
6

Choose the site and position

Goal: a ventrogluteal site you've looked at and felt, on a patient lying the right way.
Start Patient consented, still sitting up and covered.
Steps
  • Say the site and why: "Ventrogluteal, the gluteus medius: thick muscle, away from nerves and vessels, takes up to 3 mL."
  • Look at the muscle. Press on it: no tenderness or hardness. Ask about recent injections; rotate sides.
  • Look at the skin: no bruising, inflammation, edema, infection.
  • Onto their side, top knee bent forward. Uncover the hip only.
End Side-lying, knee bent, hip exposed, site named, muscle and skin checked.
Why: the bent knee relaxes the gluteus medius. A relaxed muscle is the whole reason for the position.
7

Landmark and prep the skin

Goal: a cleaned spot you found with your hand, and a needle ready in a dart grip.
Start Patient positioned. Your hands bare.
Steps
  • Hand hygiene. Clean gloves. Use the hand opposite the hip.
  • Heel of hand on the greater trochanter. Thumb toward the groin. Index finger on the anterior superior iliac spine. Middle finger back along the iliac crest. Site is the centre of the V.
  • Mark it with an alcohol wipe. Then cleanse: circles, centre outward, let it air dry.
  • Gauze between third and fourth fingers of the non-dominant hand. Cap off. Syringe between thumb and forefinger. "Try to relax."
End Site marked and dry, gauze tucked between your fingers, needle uncapped in a dart grip, patient told to relax.
Why: five bony words, said in order, are five ticks. Trochanter, groin, ASIS, crest, centre of the V.
8

Z-track injection

Goal: the drug in the muscle and sealed there, the needle made safe.
Start Needle uncapped, site clean and dry.
Steps
  • Ulnar side of the non-dominant hand below the site. Pull the skin sideways 2.5–3.5 cm and hold.
  • Grasp the body of the muscle. Dart in at 90°.
  • Keep the skin taut. Steady the barrel with that same hand resting on the patient.
  • Say: "Aspiration is only for the dorsogluteal. If blood appeared, new syringe and needle, start over."
  • Inject slowly. Count ten. Withdraw smoothly. Safety sheath on. Then release the skin.
  • Gauze on, gentle pressure, no massage. Bandage if needed.
End Needle withdrawn and sheathed, skin released after it, gauze held on the site.
Why: releasing the skin after the needle is out closes the track and keeps the drug in the muscle. That's the whole point of "Z".
9

Make them safe and sign

Goal: sharps gone, patient settled and watched, MAR signed, you coming back.
Start Gauze on the site, syringe in your hand.
Steps

  • Stay several minutes: any itching, rash, swelling, trouble breathing?
  • Items in reach. Call light, show me. Rails up, bed down. Room tidy.
  • "I'll be back in 15 to 30 minutes to check the site."
End Room safe, MAR signed, return promised. Done.
Why: "immediately" is the graded word for the MAR. Sign before you tidy, not after.

Both orders and a pain score

Steps

  • Curtain. Hand hygiene. "Hi, I'm ___, your student nurse."
  • Two identifiers against the MAR.
  • "Pain, 0 to 10?" Explain: pill first, then the dressing. "Okay with you?"
End Both orders checked, patient identified, a pain score written down, agreement given.
Why: you'll ask for the pain score again before the dressing. The second number only means something if you have the first.
2

Prepare the pill

Goal: one labelled, in-date medication in the right cup, checked twice.
Start Back at the cart.
Steps
  • Correct drug. Label to MAR: name, dose, route, time. "Second check": again.
  • Expiry out loud. Say: "A partial dose would need an independent double-check."
  • Tablet into a med cup, package kept with it. Elixir into a plastic cup, read at eye level.
  • Patient's name on the MAR to the prepared label. "CNO Medication standard." Cart locked.
End Pill in a cup with its package, two label checks done, standard named, cart locked.
Why: the package goes with the cup because the third check happens at the bedside, and you need a label to read.
3

Give the pill and leave

Goal: pill swallowed, signed for, and the patient told you'll be back when it's working.
Start At the door with the cup and MAR.
Steps
  • Hand hygiene, curtain, "it's ___ again", gloves on.
  • Two identifiers. Third check: label to MAR at the bedside.
  • Purpose, how it works, side effects, "any questions?"
  • Sit them up. Water. Stay until it's swallowed; "open your mouth for me?"
  • Sign the MAR now. "I'll give that 30 minutes to work before the dressing."
  • Call bell, bed low, tidy, gloves off, hand hygiene.
End Pill taken and documented, patient safe, you out of the room with a plan to return.
Why: step 16 is its own tick: saying out loud that you'll wait for the medication to work.
4

Come back and set up

Goal: everything within reach, patient draped and warned, bag cuffed, clean gloves on.
Start Returning with the dressing supplies.
Steps
  • Name the supplies: sterile tray, saline, extra gauze and ABD, sterile gloves, clean gloves, tape, biohazard bag, measuring guide.
  • Curtain, hand hygiene, introduce. Two identifiers against the bracelet.
  • "Pain now, 0 to 10?" Compare. Explain the dressing change. "Still okay?"
  • Say whether spray is a risk (gown, goggles, mask if so).
  • Position for the wound. Drape to show only the site. "Please don't touch the wound or the sterile supplies."
  • Biohazard bag open, top folded into a cuff, within reach. Clean gloves.
End Wound exposed, patient warned, cuffed bag beside you, clean gloves on.
Why: the cuff is so you can drop soiled things in without touching the outside of the bag.
5

Old dressing off

Goal: the wound uncovered without hurting the skin, the old dressing contained and gone.
Start Clean gloves on, bag ready.
Steps
  • Tape: pull parallel to the skin, toward the dressing, with the hair, holding the skin down. Adhesive off.
  • Outer layer first, one layer at a time. Look at the drainage: amount, colour. Keep the soiled side out of their sight.
  • Fold the dressing in on itself. Peel a glove inside out over it. Into the biohazard bag.
  • Hand hygiene.
End Wound open to air, old dressing and gloves in the bag, hands clean.
Why: the glove-over-the-dressing trick is how you contain it without a second trip.
6

Look and measure

Goal: a full description and three numbers.
Start Wound exposed, hands clean, no gloves.
Steps
  • Seven words, in order: colour, integrity, exudate, edema, approximation, odour, infection.
  • If it's an incision: are the margins approximated?
  • Measure length, width, depth.
  • Clean gloves. Palpate lightly along the incision for a healing ridge. Gloves off. Hand hygiene.
End Wound described in seven words, measured, palpated; hands clean again.
Why: the seven words are seven ticks. Say the list the same way every time so none drop out.
7

Open the sterile field and pour

Goal: a sterile field with wet gauze, a drape, extra supplies, and your sterile gloves on.
Start Tray on the table at waist height, still wrapped.
Steps
  • Flaps: far one away from you, side flaps out with your arms off the field, near flap toward you. Say the three rules: don't reach across, don't turn away, don't leave.
  • Garbage bag out with the forceps into your other hand. Forceps down, tips on the field.
  • Check the saline: contents, expiry. Basin at the edge, inside the one-inch border. Cap up, inside facing up. (Opened bottle: pour a splash out first.)
  • Label to palm. Lip 2.5–5 cm above the basin. Pour slowly. No splash.
  • Drape by its corners, next to the wound. Drop extra gauze and the ABD onto the field, wrapper never crossing it.
  • Sterile gloves.
End Open field, wet gauze, drape placed, supplies dropped in, sterile gloves on, hands above the waist.
Why: the outer inch isn't sterile. Everything you'll use lives inside it; the bag and the bottle live outside it.
8

Clean and dress

Goal: a clean, dry wound under a layered dressing, taped and labelled.
Start Sterile gloves on, field open.
Steps
  • Gauze with forceps, dip, wring out. One stroke per gauze. Least contaminated to most: the line first, then each side, outward.
  • Blot dry with dry gauze.
  • Loosely woven gauze on the wound. More gauze if needed. ABD pad on top.
  • Sterile gloves off. Hand hygiene. Clean gloves on.
  • Tape perpendicular to the wound. Label it: date, time, initials.
End New dressing taped and dated, clean gloves on.
Why: the glove switch is the line between sterile work (touching the wound) and clean work (tape and tidy).

Clean up, sign, report

Goal: supplies gone, patient safe, everything written down and passed on.
Start Dressing on, clean gloves on.
Steps
  • PPE off. Dispose of supplies and the tray. Seal the bag.
  • Comfortable. Items in reach. Call light, show me. Rails up, bed down.
  • Gloves off. Hand hygiene.
  • Document: wound description, size, drainage, dressing applied, pain before and after, tolerated. Report to the RN.
End Room tidy, note written, RN told. Done.
Why: "documented and reported" is one step with two halves. Say who you're reporting to.

1

Orders and the right patient

Goal: three orders verified and a patient who knows the plan.
Start Orders and MAR at the cart, then the doorway.
Steps
  • All three orders against the MAR: oral, patch, nebulizer.
  • Curtain. Hand hygiene. Introduce yourself to the patient and family.
  • Two identifiers against the MAR.
  • Explain all three. Be specific about what they'll do themselves (take the tablet, breathe through the mask). "Okay with you?"
End Orders checked, patient identified, plan agreed.
Why: step 5 is graded on being specific about self-administration. Say what they'll do, not just what you'll do.
2

Prepare every oral form at the cart

Goal: each medication in the right cup, checked, in date, and the rule for every form said out loud.
Start At the cart, only this patient's MAR open.
Steps
  • "One patient at a time." Correct drug. Label to MAR: name, dose, time, route. Expiry on every one.
  • Tablets in one cup, elixir in a plastic cup.
  • Half tablet: scored only, clean cutter, discard the other half.
  • Crushing: only if it can be crushed; each one separately; in soft food; given separately.
  • Liquid: cup on a level surface, read at eye level; under 10 mL goes in an oral syringe. Wipe the lip, recap.
  • Name on the MAR to every label. "CNO Medication standard." Cart locked.
End Everything prepared and labelled for one patient, every form's rule said, cart locked.
Why: you may only have a tablet in the cup, but the half-tablet, crushing and liquid rules are each their own tick. Say them anyway.
3

Bedside checks and the assessment

Goal: identity, allergies, third check, teaching, and the vital signs each drug needs.
Start At the door with the tray.
Steps
  • Curtain, hand hygiene, introduce again. Two identifiers. Allergies.
  • Third check: each label to the MAR to the patient's name.
  • For each drug: purpose, how it works, side effects. "Any questions?"
  • Assess for each: patch needs blood pressure, heart rate, chest pain. Nebulizer needs respiratory rate, breath sounds, SpO₂, pulse. The oral med needs whatever it treats.
End All checks done, patient taught, baseline numbers written down.
Why: the nebulizer's "reassess after" step only works if you have a "before".
4

Give the oral med by its route

Goal: the oral medication taken the way its form requires, and seen to be taken.
Start Patient sitting up, cup in your hand.
Steps
  • Water or their preferred drink, if it's compatible.
  • ODT: peel the foil, don't push through, on top of the tongue, no chewing.
  • Buccal: against the cheek and gums until it dissolves.
  • Sublingual: under the tongue, no chewing, no swallowing. Same for lozenges.
  • Powder: mix at the bedside, drink right away.
  • Stay until it's taken. "Open your mouth for me?"
End Oral med gone, by the right route, witnessed.
Why: four route rules, four ticks. Say the rule for each form even if you only hand over one.
5

Old patch off

Goal: yesterday's patch found, removed, folded shut, and gone.
Start Oral med done. Hands clean.
Steps
  • Hand hygiene. Clean gloves (so the nitro doesn't touch you).
  • "Where's your patch?" Check its date. Peel it off.
  • Fold it sticky sides together. Into the right bin.
  • Wipe the skin if needed; look at it. Gloves off.
End Old patch disposed of, old site clean and inspected, gloves off.
Why: folded sticky-to-sticky means no one, including a child or a pet, can touch the drug.
6

New patch on, four warnings

Goal: a dated patch on a fresh site, pressed down, and the patient told the four rules.
Start New patch still in its pouch.
Steps
  • Date, time, initials on the patch before opening.
  • Clean gloves. Pick a site: clean, dry, intact, hairless, away from yesterday's. Not oily, burned, cut or irritated.
  • Out of the cover by the edge. On immediately. Press the edges with two fingers. Don't rub.
  • Four warnings: don't cut it; no heating pad; don't reuse a site within a week; no other nitro at the same time.
  • Gloves off. Hand hygiene.
End New patch on and dated, four rules taught, hands clean.
Why: heat speeds absorption and cutting changes the dose. The four rules are all about keeping the dose what was ordered.
7

Nebulizer: build, load, run

Goal: the whole dose breathed in, with you coaching the breath and watching the pulse.
Start Hands clean, nebulizer parts on the table.
Steps
  • Hand hygiene. Gloves. Assemble per the manufacturer: tubing to air source, tubing to cup, cup upright.
  • Medication into the cup. Diluent too if the order needs it. Top on tight. Mask connected. Patient upright.
  • Airflow on. See the mist. Mask on.
  • Coach: slow deep breath, a bit more than normal, brief hold at the top, relaxed breath out, repeat until the mist stops. If short of breath: normal breaths with an occasional deep one.
  • Tap the cup now and then, and near the end. Say: "I'm monitoring your pulse throughout."
  • Mist gone: regulator off.
End Cup empty, regulator off, mask off.
Why: the breathing pattern is graded in full, including the short-of-breath version. Say both.
8

Nebulizer: after

Goal: the two after-rules said, and the equipment cleaned and stored dry.
Start Treatment finished, mask in your hand.
Steps
  • Say: "If this were a steroid, I'd have you rinse and gargle with warm water."
  • Say: "I'd reassess now, respiratory rate, breath sounds, SpO₂, pulse, and compare to my baseline."
  • Take it apart. Shake out the cup. Rinse each part in sterile water. Shake off. Air-dry. Clean bag when dry.
End Both rules said, parts drying, bag ready.
Why: steps 51 and 52 are graded only on being said. Nothing to do, just two sentences.
9

Safe, signed, noted

Goal: patient safe, each medication signed for, and a note that covers care, teaching, and tolerance.
Start Equipment drying, gloves on.
Steps
  • Supplies discarded. Gloves off. Hand hygiene.
  • Comfortable. Items in reach. Call light, show me. Rails up, bed down.
  • MAR: signed after each medication (oral, patch with site, nebulizer). Full signature and designation in the legend.
  • Note: old site, new site, precautions taught, pre and post numbers, tolerated.
End Three signatures, one note, room safe. Done.
Why: "immediately after each administration" means three separate signatures, not one at the end.
